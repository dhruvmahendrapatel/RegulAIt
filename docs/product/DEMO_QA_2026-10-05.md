## Regulation

**1. High-risk EU AI Act obligations were due 2 August 2026. Why does the demo say December 2027?**
The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 2026-07-27) moved them: Annex III
high-risk obligations now apply from 2 December 2027 and Annex I from 2 August 2028. The
prohibitions have applied since 2 February 2025 and are unchanged. Each date is source-dated in the
regulatory feed (`packages/shared/src/demo-intake/regulatory-updates.ts`, ADR-0158).

**2. What happens when someone proposes a prohibited (Article 5) system?**
The screening labels it PROHIBITED with the rule reasons and the Article 5 refusal text, at intake
and at review (ADR-0085). The proposal is not silently dropped: it goes to the independent
reviewer, whose refusal is the recorded decision and moves it to rejected (ADR-0080). The
screening informs that decision rather than blocking it — the sign-off stays a human act.

**3. Is the tier legal advice?**
No — it is a calculator over structured answers, not a lawyer (ADR-0085). The tier is computed
from the proposer's EU AI Act answers by deterministic rules, with the rule reasons shown, and
never from free text or a model.

**4. Which frameworks are covered?**
Built-in packs for the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2, HIPAA,
PCI-DSS and FINRA (`packages/shared/src/compliance-packs.ts`); custom packs can be authored.
Version-2 EU AI Act and NIST packs add bias and safety controls (ADR-0150).

**5. Do you track US state laws?**
The feed includes the Colorado AI Act as re-enacted by SB 26-189 (effective 2027-01-01) and NYC
Local Law 144 (`regulatory-updates.ts`). It is a curated, source-dated feed joined to your controls
and use cases (ADR-0158) — not a live legal-research service.

## Architecture, data and deployment

**6. Is this a GRC tool or a gateway?**
Both, on purpose: the gateway that authorises every agent, model, connector and MCP-tool call also
records what happened, and governance reads those records. That is why trust metrics are evidence
coverage rather than questionnaire scores (ADR-0148).

**7. Where does our data live?**
RegulAIt is a single-tenant deployment — one deployment serves one organisation
(`apps/gateway/src/org-settings.ts`) — with its own Postgres; your records stay in that database. Automated BYOC deployment into AWS, Azure or GCP is partial: AWS runs behind an
off-by-default flag, Azure/GCP/Kubernetes are dry-runs today (ROADMAP Batch C).

**8. Air-gapped?**
The governed core needs no outbound call: providers can be mock or your own endpoints, and every
outbound post (chat, connectors) passes an egress allow-list, so an air-gapped deployment simply
has no chat courier (ADR-0162). Packaged air-gapped install tooling is not today — ROADMAP Batch C.

**9. Which models and vendors?**
Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and xAI natively, plus custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints for open-weight
or in-house models, and a keyless mock (`packages/model-provider/src/index.ts`, ADR-0034).
Entitlement is per user and independent of vendor.

**10. Do you store prompts and responses?**
Previews are stored only as the org's content-capture setting allows, and credentials are scrubbed
at write time (ADR-0160). Continuous trace evaluation re-runs the shipped detectors over those
previews and keeps counts per detector, never the matched text.

## Discovery and monitoring

**11. How do you find shadow AI?**
By classifying evidence you import — SaaS exports, egress logs, code scans — against a provider
catalogue, with the coverage of sources shown. There is no network scanning today (ROADMAP §9,
Phase 1).

**12. How do alerts reach our SOC or Slack?**
Every alert is written to the hash-chained audit log (ADR-0157, ADR-0060), exportable as CSV or an
offline-verifiable signed bundle; a SIEM ingests those exports. Newly raised alerts can also post
to Slack or Teams above a per-workspace severity threshold (ADR-0162). There is no live SIEM push
today (ADR-0135, proposed).

**13. How often does monitoring run?**
The governance monitor runs hourly and on demand ("Evaluate now"); trace evaluation runs every 15
minutes (ADR-0157, ADR-0160). Alerts raise, refresh and resolve as conditions change, and an
acknowledgement survives refreshes.

**14. What does the trust dashboard measure?**
For each of six dimensions, the share of applicable active-pack controls with evidence (ADR-0148).
A dimension with no applicable control is unmeasured — shown as a gap, never zero; one whose
control has no evidence is measured at 0 % (ADR-0150).

**15. Our cost optimizer routes to cheaper models. Will governance notice?**
Yes: if an approved use case's traffic is served by an agent its approval does not name, the
monitor raises a high alert with the measured call count (ADR-0164). It changes no routing — you
decide to pin the traffic or extend the approval.

## Approvals and control

**16. Who approves a new AI use case?**
The intake workflow's sign-off goes to a named governance approver (ADR-0080, ADR-0165), so the
proposer cannot approve their own registration. Toxic role combinations are refused at the grant
choke point (ADR-0091), and every decision is one audited row.

**17. Does RegulAIt fix risks automatically?**
No. The planner proposes remediations; the executable ones (link a control, assign an owner) run
only after a different human — not the proposer — approves them, and everything else is guidance
(ADR-0159).

**18. Can a developer bypass governance?**
With key custody enforced, user-supplied vendor credentials are refused at dispatch, so calls go
through RegulAIt's governed path (ADR-0024). Network-level egress enforcement is an
infrastructure control, not something the product enforces today (ROADMAP item 15, Batch C).

**19. How does this plug into CI/CD?**
A pipeline step calls `POST /v1/gates/deploy`; it denies when the use case is not approved, an
agent is outside the approved stack, halted, refused by the model-risk gate, or has an open high
alert (ADR-0161). It always checks the use case's full approved stack, whatever agents the
pipeline names; naming fewer cannot skip one. It enforces only where the pipeline calls it, and
every answer is audited with the build ref.

**20. What evidence do auditors get?**
A hash-chained audit trail anchored to WORM storage (ADR-0060), exportable as a signed bundle that
verifies offline. Use-case decisions, alert episodes, remediation approvals and gate answers are
all rows in that one trail.

## The demo itself

**21. What was mocked?**
The model provider (keyless mock agents) and the shadow-AI evidence (a seeded import). The
governed traffic is real dispatches through the product's one path (ADR-0163), and every page,
metric, alert and approval is live product code over the seeded database.

**22. Can we see it on our own data?**
Yes — the same commands seed an empty database (`demo:prepare`), and a proof of concept imports
your own evidence and registers your systems. The environment runs where you run it (Q7).
