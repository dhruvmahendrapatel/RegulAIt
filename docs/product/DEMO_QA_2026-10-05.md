# AI Intake Demo Q&A / Objection Handling
**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Audience:** CISO / CRO / Head of AI Governance

## 1. Regulatory & Compliance
**Q: The EU AI Act obligations don't apply until 2026 for most systems. Why are you showing Dec 2027?**
A: The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) amended the original timeline. High-risk obligations for Annex III systems now apply from 2 December 2027, and for Annex I systems from 2 August 2028. RegulAIt's regulatory intelligence feed is updated to reflect these current dates (see `packages/shared/src/demo-intake/regulatory-updates.ts`).

**Q: Does RegulAIt automatically fix the risks it finds?**
A: No, RegulAIt does not automatically fix risks. We automate the discovery, mapping, and monitoring of risks, but remediation and approval are always human-gated. Nothing changes the governed state until a human approves it, as documented in **ADR-0159**.

## 2. Architecture & Data Residency
**Q: Is this a GRC tool or an API gateway?**
A: It is both, working in tandem. RegulAIt connects the governance plane directly to the execution layer via its gateway. We don't rely solely on static attestations; we check the gateway logs and actual configurations to provide evidence-based governance, as outlined in **ADR-0148**.

**Q: Where does our data live? Do you support BYOC (Bring Your Own Cloud) or on-premise deployments?**
A: Not today — roadmap §3 Batch C covers infra-ops and BYOC real execution. Currently, BYOC cloud execution is built as a dry-run shape. The real lazy-SDK clients exist behind a default-off flag, pending further productionization.

**Q: How do governance alerts reach our security operations center (SOC) or Slack?**
A: Not today — SIEM export and CSV audit paths are defined in the roadmap (Batch D, ADR-0012 deferrals). Currently, alerts are viewed and managed within the RegulAIt governance monitor dashboard (**ADR-0157**).

## 3. Models, Vendors, and Integration
**Q: Which models and vendors do you support?**
A: We support any publicly available model through a neutral interface. The gateway currently resolves Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI, and mock providers natively (see `packages/model-provider/src/index.ts`). RegulAIt never hard-locks to one vendor at any layer.

**Q: How do you integrate with our existing CI/CD pipelines?**
A: We provide a CI/CD deploy gate that enforces compliance programmatically. A pipeline step can call our API (`POST /v1/gates/deploy`), which will block the release if there are unacknowledged high-severity alerts (**ADR-0161**).

**Q: What parts of the demo are "mocked" versus live?**
A: The model provider is a keyless mock (`mock-balanced`), and the initial Shadow AI discovery event is a seeded artifact. However, the API endpoints, the risk math (**ADR-0147**), and the trust metrics derived from seeded ledgers are fully live and query the actual database.

## 4. Approvals and Workflows
**Q: Who approves the deployment of a new AI use case?**
A: Approvals enforce a separation of duties. A risk reviewer or approver (who cannot be the proposer) must review the residual risk and linked controls. Their approval writes an immutable audit record that gates the deployment.

**Q: Can a developer bypass the governance checks?**
A: If the organization enforces key custody—meaning developers are only issued RegulAIt keys rather than raw vendor keys—governance cannot be bypassed. Unmapped or unresolvable models default to deny (**ADR-0020**).
